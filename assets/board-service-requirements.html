Serving on the Board

What it takes to serve—and what the club will expect of you.

Before you say yes

  • Make time for meetings, preparation and assigned work.
  • Be willing to ask questions and make decisions together.
  • Protect private information and disclose conflicts.
  • Help with club events and fundraising.

You do not need to know everything on day one. You do need to learn, communicate and follow through.

Can I serve?
  • Membership: Eligible voting full member in good standing, with one completed year of active full membership by taking office.
  • Renewal: Meet the applicable renewal qualification.
  • Attendance: At least 75% of general meetings actually held from January 1 through the close of nominations, rounded up. This also applies to Lifer and Past President candidates.
  • Commitment: Demonstrate leadership and support the club’s purposes.

The Secretary verifies qualifications from official records. Appointees meet the same attendance qualification through appointment consideration. A documented attendance hardship exception requires the bylaw vote; it does not waive the one-year membership rule.

Time and attendance
  • Board meetings: Normally the last Tuesday of each month.
  • General meetings: Normally the first Tuesday. Confirm announced times and locations.
  • Between meetings: Read reports, respond to club communications and finish the work you accept.
  • Events: Help with assigned rides, fundraisers and community activities.

Plan for meeting time plus preparation and tasks. Officer duties and major events can take more time; there is no hourly minimum created by this guide.

Notify both the President and Vice President at least 24 hours before an absence when practicable, or as soon as possible in a true emergency. Attendance review and any resulting action follow the adopted bylaws.

Your work as a director
  • Read the agenda, budget and supporting reports before voting.
  • Ask what a proposal will accomplish, what it costs and who will carry it out.
  • Cast your own Board vote. A director cannot give that vote to a proxy.
  • Lead or support assigned committees, projects and events.
  • Report progress early and ask for help before a deadline is missed.

Example: When a member requests assistance, use the approved process, confirm available funds, disclose any conflict and record the authorized decision before promising payment.

Conduct and confidentiality
  • Disagree respectfully. Avoid personal attacks, threats, intimidation and disruption.
  • Follow meeting procedures and support authorized decisions.
  • Protect private member, Board and financial information during and after service.
  • Disclose personal, family, business or financial conflicts and follow recusal rules.
  • Use approved club accounts and protect records and access.

Confidentiality does not prevent required disclosures, good-faith reports to appropriate authorities or confidential professional advice. Corrective action follows the bylaws and fair process.

Money and fundraising
  • Follow spending and contract approval limits.
  • Keep receipts, protect designated funds and document money transfers.
  • Do not make unauthorized commitments in the club’s name.
  • Support fundraising through time, promotion and practical work.
  • Complete any required conflict-of-interest disclosure.

Personal contributions are encouraged when able. This guide creates no required donation amount.

Offices and handover

Election to the Board gives you a director seat. Officer selection is a separate process; no particular title is guaranteed.

  • President: Chairs meetings and coordinates authorized operations.
  • Vice President: Supports the President and provides continuity.
  • Secretary: Keeps minutes, notices and official records.
  • Treasurer: Maintains financial records, receipts, reports and budgets.
  • Sergeant at Arms: Supports meeting order and safety within approved authority.
  • Directors: Share voting responsibility and support assigned work.

Ordinary directors serve staggered two-year terms beginning January 1. Confirm the actual seat and term on the election record, including any shorter unexpired term. The President serves two years; the other four core officers serve one year under the current portal bylaws. Core officer qualifications and exceptions follow the bylaws.

When your role ends, transfer essential access at succession and remaining club records and property within seven calendar days using a jointly acknowledged handover checklist. Document external provider delays.

After you are elected
  1. Confirm your seat and term dates with the Secretary.
  2. Read the current bylaws, operating policies, recent minutes and budget.
  3. Complete your own online Board acceptance. Returning directors complete a fresh annual acknowledgment too.
  4. Review open work and records with the outgoing director or officer.
  5. Agree on your first assignments and who will help you learn the role.

If workload or availability changes, tell the President or an appropriate unconflicted director promptly. Any resignation, review or removal follows the bylaws.

The adopted bylaws and operating policies control. This guide explains Board service; it does not change qualifications, authority or removal procedures.
