Our governing documents give everyone a common reference. The bylaws explain the club's authority and members' rights. Operating Policies explain how procedures work. The Code of Conduct explains our expectations for behavior.

Prospects receive access when they become patched members. Once you have that access, read the current documents and ask about anything you do not understand.

You are not expected to quote every rule from memory. You are expected to know where the documents are, check them when needed, and follow what applies to you. Saying you did not memorize something does not remove that responsibility.

Acknowledging the Code means accepting responsibility for following it. We want everyone to understand those expectations and know how to get an answer when unsure. Here is what the Code asks of us.

Respect and Brotherhood/Sisterhood

Treat all members and associates respectfully and courteously, regardless of rank or status.
Uphold the values of loyalty, camaraderie, and trust within the club.
Resolve conflicts amicably and privately, seeking guidance from leadership if needed.
Respect differences in political, religious, and personal beliefs.
Harassment, discrimination, or bullying will not be tolerated.

Commitment and Participation

Strive to attend all scheduled meetings, rides, and events whenever possible. 80-85%
Actively participate in club activities and support charitable events and community outreach.
Represent the club proudly by wearing club colors/patches during official events.
Meet attendance expectations established in the club bylaws.

Lawful and Safe Riding

Always follow all traffic laws and ride safely to protect yourself and others.
Adhere to the club's riding formation and safety protocols during group rides.
Avoid reckless or irresponsible riding. – Over revving of engine after drinking and speeding!!
Remain quiet and attentive during all pre-ride briefings.
Do not operate a motorcycle while impaired by alcohol, illegal drugs, or medications that affect safe operation.

Confidentiality

Keep club business, votes, disciplinary matters, and other sensitive matters private.
Protect the club's reputation by refraining from gossip, slander, or defamation.

No Violence or Criminal Activity

Avoid engaging in violence, intimidation, illegal activities, or conduct that brings discredit upon the club.
Any violation may result in removal.

Chain of Command

Respect the club's leadership and follow their directives.
Bring grievances, concerns, or suggestions through the established chain of command.

Support for Members

Assist fellow members during illness, injury, funerals, family emergencies, roadside breakdowns, and other times of need.
Foster a supportive and inclusive environment.

Club Property and Colors

Treat all club property with respect.
Return all club-owned property when leaving.

Representation

Represent the club positively in public and online.
Avoid engaging in social media disputes, sharing internal club business publicly, or posting content that could harm the club's image.
Be respectful when posting photos or content involving fellow members.

Community Service

Members are encouraged to support charitable events and community outreach. Giving back is a defining value of the club.

Official Communications

Official club announcements will be made through approved communication channels. Members are responsible for staying informed.

Prospect Expectations

Prospects are expected to learn club customs, participate in club activities, demonstrate respect, and follow officer guidance.

Club Unity

Members shall not intentionally create unnecessary conflict, division, or hostility that negatively impacts the club.

The next few slides apply those expectations to personal boundaries, privacy, and the way we handle a concern.

One important part of respect is honoring personal boundaries. If someone declines an advance, stop. Do not keep asking, send unwanted messages, touch them without permission, or make them uncomfortable for saying no.

This applies to members, guests, partners, staff, and people we meet at events. Alcohol does not excuse unwanted behavior. Someone should be able to enjoy being around the club without having to manage repeated pressure.

If you witness a concern, help the person get support and use the reporting process. Taking care of each other includes respecting a boundary the first time it is expressed.

Club information deserves careful handling. Member details, financial records, complaints, and internal decisions should stay with the people who have an authorized reason to receive them.

Before forwarding a message or screenshot, think about who is in it and whether you have permission to share it. Something sent privately can still end up in a public conversation.

Protecting confidentiality does not prevent good-faith reports to appropriate authorities, required disclosures, or confidential professional advice. It helps us handle ordinary club business responsibly and protect the people involved. When you are unsure whether information should be shared, ask first.

When leadership raises a concern, take it seriously and listen to what needs to be addressed. The person involved should have notice of the concern and a meaningful opportunity to respond.

A fair review considers the available facts, avoids conflicts of interest, and follows the governing process. A complaint needs examination, and immediate safety concerns may require action while that examination continues.

Cooperate with the review and understand any resulting decision, expectations, and available appeal route. Correcting a problem early helps protect everyone involved. The same need for fairness and clear decisions also explains why our meetings follow an organized process.

Robert's Rules gives groups a shared way to conduct business. Henry Martyn Robert, an Army officer, developed the guide after struggling to lead a meeting. The first edition appeared in 1876.

For our purposes, the idea is straightforward: people take turns, discuss the actual question, and reach a clear decision. Everyone should be able to follow what the group is deciding.

You do not need to master parliamentary language for this course. Our governing documents take priority where they specify a procedure. What we need from members is attention, patience, and a willingness to let the meeting move in an orderly way.

During a meeting, face the speaker and keep side conversations down. If you are talking over someone, the people around you may miss information they need.

Use the chair's process to ask a question or raise a proposal. Explain what you want the club to consider, and keep discussion focused on that question. When a vote is required, the proper voters decide and the result goes into the record.

A personal concern that does not belong in the group discussion can be raised privately afterward. This helps the meeting stay useful while still giving people a way to get an answer.

If an ordinary concern remains unresolved, you can request Board review through the President. Explain to the President what happened, what remains unanswered, and what you are asking the Board to consider.

A clear request gives directors something they can evaluate. Bring relevant information and explain the outcome you want. Avoid turning the matter into a running argument in GroupMe or a campaign for people to take sides.

The Board will consider the issue within its authority and the governing process. A complaint involving the President uses a separate route so the person named in the complaint does not control their own review.

For an ordinary complaint, come to the President through the established process. Give specific facts and any available evidence so the concern can be addressed responsibly.

If the complaint involves the President, bring it to the Vice President for the full Board's attention. If that person is also involved, use an unconflicted director. Protect the privacy of the people concerned and avoid spreading the story around the club.

Retaliation for a good-faith report is unacceptable. If there is immediate danger, get appropriate help promptly. Respect for leadership includes having a fair way to raise a serious concern about leadership itself.

GroupMe, official email, and our approved announcements help us keep people informed. Read the whole message before replying, including the date, time, location, and any instructions.

When someone asks for a response, answer so the organizer can plan. If your availability changes, let the right person know. Do not assume another member will repeat every announcement or remind you personally.

Keep unrelated conversation from burying important event details. Take personal disagreements to a direct conversation or the proper review process. Staying informed is a shared responsibility, and a little attention saves volunteers a lot of unnecessary follow-up.

If someone criticizes BSSC on Facebook or elsewhere online, pause before responding. Send the President the link or a screenshot so leadership can decide whether an official response is needed.

A comment can travel far beyond the people who originally saw it. Screenshots may leave out the context, and personal posts can still be connected to the club's name you wear.

Do not share confidential business or make promises for BSSC in a comment thread. You can protect the club by getting information to the right person and allowing the authorized spokesperson to handle the response. Let us practice that choice.
