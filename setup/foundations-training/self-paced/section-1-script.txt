First, put yourself at ease. This course is not a disciplinary meeting. Taking it does not mean that you are in trouble or that the Board is angry with you. It establishes the same foundation for every person who joins the club.

The Board and President noticed recurring misunderstandings and took an honest look at their own responsibilities. Members deserve to understand an expectation, the reason behind it, and how to follow it. The President shares responsibility for making that guidance clear. Every prospect must complete BSSC Foundations before patching in as a full member, with the understanding and support needed to succeed.

We need a strong Board that communicates clearly and supports its members. We need a strong membership that helps each other and follows through. Every member and prospect has a part in making that happen.

Welcome to BSSC Foundations. This course connects the pieces of being a BoneShaker: where we came from, what we stand for, and how we work together.

Some of you are prospecting, and some have already earned your cut. Wherever you are in that process, you should leave with a clearer understanding of the club and your place in it. Note anything that needs clarification and contact club leadership afterward. You can revisit the lessons and transcript; you do not need to memorize every detail in one sitting.

We will begin with our history, because our expectations make more sense when you understand the people and purpose behind them.

Our story begins with friendship and a love of riding. This picture takes us back to January 2005.

In the very middle is our very own Godfather Gordon, an original charter member who is still active in the club. He gives us a living connection to those early days.

Let’s look next at where those friends came together and how BoneShakers became a club of its own.

In 2005, about twelve friends came together at the original BoneShakers Saloon and formed BoneShakers Social Club. Riding and friendship brought them together. 

Helping people in the community became part of what they wanted to accomplish.

The saloon is an important part of our beginning, but the club had its own identity. A meeting place can change. The organization, its members, and its responsibilities continue.

We are carrying forward something people built together. Next, let’s talk about our founding President, Big John.

Our founding President was John Wilkerson, known as Big John. He helped bring the original group together and gave the club an early sense of direction.

The history handed down to us describes someone who cared about his community. Friendship and riding mattered, and so did using that shared interest to help local people and charities. Those priorities are still part of why we are here.

Remembering Big John helps us understand the responsibility that passes from one group of members to the next. We inherit the opportunity to enjoy this club and the responsibility to take care of it.

Big Lick is the name connected to our club logo, and its story starts with a tattoo John already had from his years in Roanoke. Roanoke was once known as Big Lick, a name associated with the area's natural salt deposits.

That tattoo became the starting point for the club's original logo. So when you hear us talk about Big Lick, you are hearing a connection to our beginning in 2005.

The workbench scene illustrates that origin story. On the next slide, you will see the original artwork alongside the later design, showing how that connection carried forward.

In 2025, BoneShakers reached its twentieth anniversary. The artwork on this slide connects that milestone to our beginning in 2005.

The original Big Lick and the renewed design look different, but they belong to the same club story. Twenty years together represents people showing up, friendships growing, leadership changing, and community work continuing.

An anniversary gives us a chance to appreciate that history and think about what we are handing to the next group of members. We honor the people who came before us through the way we represent the club and treat one another today.

The founders chose to build a Social Club. That identity gives us room for riders and nonriders, different backgrounds, and different ways of contributing.

You do not have to own a motorcycle to have a place here. You can enjoy the friendships, help at events, support community projects, and be someone other members can count on.

We want people to enjoy being BoneShakers. Clear expectations and dependable leadership help that happen because people know how things work and what they can expect from each other. Fellowship and good times belong alongside our responsibilities and the service we provide.

Giving back was part of the club's purpose from the beginning. People combined their time, contacts, and interest in riding to help local charities and people facing difficult circumstances.

Bikes for Tykes became an important part of that work, but our concern for the community reaches beyond one program. Sometimes we can provide money. Other times we can offer volunteer help, useful contacts, or support for another organization's effort.

The important connection is that we accomplish more by following through together. Our charitable work grows out of the relationships we build and the effort members are willing to share.

Bikes for Tykes makes that purpose easy to see. The 2026 goal was one hundred bicycles, one hundred helmets, and one hundred locks: eighty sets for the YMCA and twenty for Courthouse Horizons.

Each set represents a child and a family. Reaching that child takes much more than collecting a donation. Someone orders the bikes, receives them, assembles them, checks them, and gets them to the right place.

Members, donors, volunteers, and partners all contribute. That shared effort is something to take pride in, and it gives us a responsibility to protect the money and promises connected to the program.

We would like to help every deserving person who comes to us, but we have to work within our resources and our approved budget.

We track the General Fund and Bikes for Tykes funds separately. A bank balance can include money reserved for bikes, existing obligations, or other planned expenses. The number in the account does not tell you how much we can freely spend.

Before making a financial commitment, we need the Treasurer's information and the proper approval. When money is unavailable, we can still consider other ways to help. Being responsible today protects our ability to keep helping tomorrow.
