The Road Captain organizes the plan for a group ride. That includes the route, timing, stops, and the information riders need before departure.

Pay attention during the briefing. Understand the formation, signals, regrouping plan, and what to do if the route changes. Asking before we leave is much easier than trying to work something out while moving.

The Road Captain coordinates the ride with the Tail Gunner and other assigned support. Each rider still controls their own motorcycle and remains responsible for following traffic laws and riding within their ability. A clear plan works when the group understands and follows it.

The Tail Gunner takes the assigned rear position and helps the Road Captain know what is happening behind the group. That may include separation, a stopped rider, or a breakdown.

Know who is filling the role before departure. Listen to the assistance plan and understand how you should communicate if you need to stop or leave.

The Tail Gunner helps coordinate a response but cannot automatically see or solve every problem. Unexpected moves make the job harder and can put people at risk. Use the agreed signals and plan so the group has a chance to respond safely.

The pre-ride briefing deserves everyone’s attention. Stop side conversations and listen, even if you have ridden the route before. Know the destination, planned stops, regrouping plan, signals, and safe way to leave the ride. Ask questions before departure.

We ride single file and maintain steady, consistent spacing. Stay aware of the motorcycle ahead and the riders behind you. Avoid unnecessary gaps that stretch out the group, while keeping enough distance to brake, react, and maneuver safely. Maintain at least a two-second following distance and increase it for poor visibility, wet roads, or other hazards.

Ride smoothly and predictably. Avoid sudden acceleration, unnecessary braking, showing off, or pressuring another rider. The ride leader should set a manageable pace so the group can stay together safely.

If a vehicle enters the group, give it room and rebuild the formation when safe. If you become separated, follow the agreed regrouping plan. Never race to catch up or take a risk just to keep the group together.

A disciplined group earns respect through preparation, control, and good judgment.

Before drinking, decide how you are getting home. If someone is impaired, riding or driving is not a safe option.

Look out for each other. Help arrange another way home and involve the appropriate ride or club leadership. Keep the conversation respectful but take the concern seriously. An awkward conversation is worth having when someone's safety is involved.

Here is a situation to consider: a member has had too much to drink and insists they are fine to ride. 

Accepting help and stepping in for a friend are both part of taking care of the group.

An event runs well when people know their assignments before the crowd arrives. Ticket sales, merchandise, raffles, prize release, setup, and breakdown each need clear responsibility.

Know your assigned arrival time and check in with the coordinator. Setup access and volunteer reporting times may be different, so use the instructions for your role rather than assuming an earlier arrival is always useful.

Find out where your supplies are, what the job involves, and who handles questions. Ask before taking over a station or changing a process. Clear directions help volunteers succeed and keep a small misunderstanding from becoming an event-wide problem.

When you accept an event assignment, other people plan around that commitment. Arrive at the agreed time, check in, and be ready to do the work you accepted.

If you need to leave or step away, tell the coordinator and arrange relief. Do not assume another volunteer will notice an empty station and take over. Make a clear handoff of money, keys, supplies, and any unfinished work.

Stay through the portion of setup or breakdown you agreed to cover. If circumstances change, communicate early. Dependability makes volunteering less stressful and shows respect for the people giving their time beside you.

Our donors and community partners help us accomplish things we could not manage alone. Their experience with us influences whether they want to help again.

Thank people, honor the agreements we have made, and give credit accurately. If someone asks about a sponsorship benefit, a donation, or official club participation, take the request to leadership before promising anything.

Direct official media questions and public statements to the President. A friendly conversation can build a relationship without committing the whole club. We want our partners to know that when BSSC makes an approved promise, they can rely on us to follow through.
