When you wear our colors, people connect your actions with BoneShakers. They may never learn your name, but they will remember how someone wearing our patch treated them.

That includes a server, a donor, another rider, or a family at an event. Courtesy and good judgment strengthen the work every other member is doing. An argument or careless comment can affect people who were never involved.

Think about the club name you are carrying before you act. Looking out for our reputation begins with ordinary choices, and one of the most visible choices is how we wear and care for our colors.

Every club-related patch has a purpose. Membership colors, sister patches, recognition patches, and ordinary merchandise do not all carry the same meaning or permissions.

Before adding a patch, changing its placement, or telling someone they can wear one, ask leadership. A gift or a well-meant suggestion does not automatically make a patch or placement approved.

You are responsible for understanding what you wear. You do not need every answer memorized, but you do need to ask before guessing. Keeping our appearance consistent shows respect for the club and for the people who earned the recognition those patches represent.

Club-issued membership patches belong to BSSC. The club entrusts them to you to wear, and you must return them when our governing documents require it. Any approved exceptions for eligible Life Members and Past Presidents still apply. The vest itself and ordinary merchandise are separate from club-owned insignia.

When we are out representing the club together, put your cut on and keep it on. Do not drape it over a chair, leave it at a table, or expect someone else to watch it.

Keep your cut secure and take responsibility for it throughout the activity. Caring for it includes how it looks.

We have received criticism about the way some of our cuts looked. That is uncomfortable to hear, but it gives us a clear opportunity to improve.

Look at the comparison. Uneven placement, mismatched styles, and patches that appear careless create an impression about the whole organization. Clean material, secure stitching, and neat alignment show that we take pride in what we wear.

Before you go out, take a moment to look at your cut. If something needs attention, address it. Ask leadership before changing club-related patches. We want the care we put into our appearance to match the care we put into the club.

BoneShakers is its own organization. We make decisions through our own governing documents and leadership, while also taking part in the wider motorcycle community.

Other clubs see how we ride, speak, and behave. Treat their members and Presidents with courtesy. If you visit an event, be a respectful guest and follow the directions of the people hosting it.

Enjoy building relationships, but bring official invitations, joint activities, and proposed commitments to our leadership. An individual member cannot promise BSSC's participation on everyone's behalf. Understanding who we are helps us represent ourselves accurately when we meet other organizations.

You will hear the letters MC, RC, and SC around the motorcycle community. MC stands for Motorcycle Club. RC stands for Riding Club. SC stands for Social Club.

Broadly speaking, an MC centers its identity on motorcycles and club membership. An RC usually emphasizes organized riding. An SC emphasizes fellowship and shared activities. Individual clubs have their own traditions and requirements, so those labels do not tell you everything about a group.

Our name is BoneShakers Social Club. Riding is important to many of us, and riders and nonriders both belong. Community service and friendship are central to our identity.

Introduce us by our full name: BoneShakers Social Club. That tells a business, another club, or a prospective member who they are meeting.

Our identity brings together fellowship and community service, with riders and nonriders contributing side by side. We intend to carry that identity forward.

Members cannot independently rename BSSC, present us as an MC or RC, announce another BSSC chapter, or promise an affiliation. Questions about our local standing, another group's recognition, or future organizations belong with leadership. Bring those conversations to the President rather than making statements or commitments for the club. Accurate introductions help us build respectful relationships.

BoneShakers has increased its presence with other clubs and received positive comments about how members ride together and represent the organization.

That progress comes from people showing up, following the ride plan, and treating others well. It belongs to the whole club. A positive experience with one member can make someone more comfortable supporting our next event or welcoming us back.

We should appreciate that progress and keep earning it. Relationships need continued care. The way we act at the next ride, meeting, or public event will help determine the impression we leave.

When we are out together, people should see a club that handles itself with composure. Public arguments and insults can undo the good impression everyone else is trying to make.

If you disagree with a decision, use the club's process to raise it. Avoid turning a restaurant, event, or group chat into the place where an internal disagreement plays out.

Keeping ordinary club problems inside does not mean concealing misconduct or ignoring danger. Get appropriate help when needed. For routine concerns, bring the issue to the right person so it can be addressed with facts. That is where organized leadership matters.

Internal discussions, disagreements, and members’ personal information deserve discretion. Do not share screenshots or repeat private club conversations outside the club. Bring routine concerns to club leadership through the proper process. Keeping club business private never means concealing misconduct, ignoring danger, or preventing someone from seeking appropriate help.

Example

A member of another club says, “You raised over $20,000 for Bikes for Tykes? That’s impressive! How did you get Elevation 27? Who’s your contact, and what kind of arrangement did they give you?”
It’s fine to celebrate our success and thank our supporters. Our private arrangements, fundraising plans, and business contacts stay within BoneShakers. Members have spent time building those relationships, and we respect that work by letting club leadership handle those conversations.

A friendly response:

“That event took a lot of teamwork, and we’re proud of what it means for the kids. Our President handles the partnerships and arrangements. You’re welcome to reach out to him.”
