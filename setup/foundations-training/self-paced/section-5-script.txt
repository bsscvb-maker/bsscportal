Prospecting gives a person time to learn the club and gives the club time to get to know that person. Participation, respectful behavior, and completing the admission process all matter.

A sponsor should make introductions, explain expectations, and help the prospect find ways to take part. Prospects should ask questions and follow through rather than waiting for someone to explain everything twice.

After full membership, the responsibilities continue. Keep contact details current, complete required renewal paperwork and acknowledgments, and meet dues requirements unless an approved exemption applies. Earning the cut begins an ongoing commitment to the club.

Participation keeps members connected and helps share the work. Know the requirements for your membership status and check your attendance record during the year.

If an event or meeting is missing from your record, raise it promptly while the details are easy to confirm. Waiting until the end of the year makes corrections harder.

Work, health, and family circumstances can affect anyone. If you are having difficulty participating, talk to the President early so we can use the appropriate hardship review process. Different membership statuses have different requirements, and anyone holding an assigned role still needs to carry out that role's responsibilities.

People contribute in different ways. Someone who cannot lift bicycles may be excellent at welcoming guests, making calls, checking people in, or organizing records.

If you need an adjustment or help finding a suitable task, speak with the President privately. We can do more when we match the work to the person rather than assuming everyone can do the same thing.

Our Good Will Ambassador helps people feel welcome and connects guests and newer members with the right person. Every member can help by making an introduction and including someone who is standing on the edge of the group. That sense of belonging matters.
